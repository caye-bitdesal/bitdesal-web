**AI agents for companies** are software with one clear job. They read what comes in (message, email, voice note, photo of a delivery note), pick the next step using written rules, and act or pass it to you. In plumbing, electrical work, renovations, or installs they do not go to site for you. They cut the paperwork: copying measurements, asking again for photos, retyping the same quote. We explain the basics on [AI agents for business](/agentes-ia-empresas/); here are **six examples** we see often, with no client names and no made-up figures.

Spain’s [INE press release of 22 October 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/es/ETICCE20251T2026.htm) says 33.6% of companies with ten or more staff already used some artificial intelligence in Q1 2026. Lots of people ask about it; what usually fails is fit with how you really work. An agent helps when input is messy (an audio, a half-done WhatsApp) and you need a clear result (a draft, a record, an alert), not when “we are open 8 to 6” is enough.

<h2 id="what-is" class="bf-section-heading">
  <span class="bf-section-num" aria-hidden="true">01</span>
  <span class="bf-section-title"><span class="bf-section-lead">What is an AI agent for companies</span> <span class="bf-section-accent">and what is it not?</span></span>
</h2>

An **AI agent for companies** looks at what you receive, understands what is being asked, and can only check what we allowed (prices, calendar, whether a quote is still open). Then it does **one thing**: a short reply, a draft in your template, or an alert to someone on the team.

It is not a computer boss or a machine that knows everything. It does not run the company. It does the job we agreed in the study and stops when it should: amount above what you agreed, complaint, unknown data, or a customer who wants a person.

Before we link WhatsApp or folders with customer data, we tell you what information leaves your systems, which online service gets it, and under which contract. The European Commission summarises the rules on its [AI regulatory framework page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai). The full legal text of [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689) is on EUR-Lex.

<h2 id="agent-chatbot-auto" class="bf-section-heading">
  <span class="bf-section-num" aria-hidden="true">02</span>
  <span class="bf-section-title"><span class="bf-section-lead">Agent, chatbot, or automation:</span> <span class="bf-section-accent">what fits trades?</span></span>
</h2>

On the first call people often mix the three up. They are not the same, and picking wrong costs money.

| What | What it does | When it fits trades |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Automation** | Moves data the same way every time: from job report to alert, from sheet to order. | When the step is always the same and data is already in fields. |
| **Chatbot** | Replies with text. Does not build the quote or order from the supplier. | Hours, papers to send, questions that repeat every week. |
| **AI agent** | Reads, picks the next step, does something, or stops and passes it on. | When an audio, half-finished WhatsApp, or delivery-note photo needs **real work** done with it. |

Sometimes you need a mix: the agent understands the message, [AI process automation](/automatizacion-procesos-ia/) moves data to the right place, and if needed a short reply goes to the customer. We decide that in the study, not in a glossy sales deck.

<h2 id="six-cases" class="bf-section-heading">
  <span class="bf-section-num" aria-hidden="true">03</span>
  <span class="bf-section-title"><span class="bf-section-lead">What real AI agent cases</span> <span class="bf-section-accent">do we see in trade companies?</span></span>
</h2>

We do not publish client names. What follows is **work we actually do**, told straight. If yours looks similar, the €350 + VAT process study says whether it pays; if not, we say that clearly too.

<h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-foreground mt-8 mb-3">1. Quote from a voice note</h3>

You get back from site and record measurements and what you saw. The agent turns the audio into text, puts lines in your template, and leaves a **draft for you to price**. It does not send the quote to the customer; you send it when the total is right. It usually stops if a catalog item has several options or the total goes over the limit you set.

<h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-foreground mt-8 mb-3">2. WhatsApp: answer the repeat and collect data</h3>

The same “do you cover this area?” again. The agent answers what you already know and asks for what is missing to quote: address, photos, measurements, when you can visit. Twenty loose messages become one record. If they want a discount or a fixed date, it does not guess: **it alerts you**.

<h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-foreground mt-8 mb-3">3. Read delivery notes and match the order</h3>

A photo of the delivery note or a supplier PDF arrives. We pull references and amounts and compare them to the order. Matches are ready for your back office; mismatches are flagged for someone this week, not at month end.

<h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-foreground mt-8 mb-3">4. Quote follow-up without chasing threads</h3>

Quotes are waiting but opening WhatsApp feels like a chore. The agent writes the reminder in your tone, on the channel you already use, and notes replies. It does not push people who said no. You see an open list, not forty lost chats.

<h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-foreground mt-8 mb-3">5. Job report from the phone</h3>

**Example:** three photos and an audio from the van with work done and material used. The agent sorts that into the fields you use for invoicing or warranty and flags odd bits (missing serial, hours that do not add up). Office staff review before end of day. If your report is always the same PDF, plain automation without an agent may be enough; the study shows which.

<h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-foreground mt-8 mb-3">6. Appointments and visits from messages</h3>

**Example:** “Can you come Thursday afternoon?” It checks the calendar we linked, suggests times by area, visit length, and who can go, and leaves the visit as **draft** or asks the customer to confirm. It does not fill the diary with jobs you later move by hand.

<h2 id="limits" class="bf-section-heading">
  <span class="bf-section-num" aria-hidden="true">04</span>
  <span class="bf-section-title"><span class="bf-section-lead">How do we supervise an agent</span> <span class="bf-section-accent">without rereading everything?</span></span>
</h2>

The rule is simple: **the software suggests, a person confirms**. No quote draft, order, or reply that sets price or date goes out alone in the first days until we know the error level is OK for you.

Supervising is not reading every message again. It is checking what the system flagged and sampling the rest. If that takes as long as doing it by hand, the agent is not ready and we do not call it done.

Caye Ruiz has seen it in software projects: a “simple” feature can eat hours without a written definition of “finished.” We tell that story in [Insomnia, AI agents, and the “simple” feature](/en/blog/insomnio-agentes-ia-funcion-sencilla/). On site the worry is not code but **a wrong price or date**; stop rules use your numbers, not a random limit from the internet.

<h2 id="when-not" class="bf-section-heading">
  <span class="bf-section-num" aria-hidden="true">06</span>
  <span class="bf-section-title"><span class="bf-section-lead">When is an AI agent</span> <span class="bf-section-accent">not worth it?</span></span>
</h2>

We say this on the first call when it applies:

- **Low volume:** the job happens once a month and takes twenty minutes; you will not earn back the build cost.
- **Everything changes every week:** without a fixed template we tidy the process first; the agent comes after.
- **You already solved it:** if software you use does the job, we say so and do not build another one.
- **You want it to negotiate price alone:** we do not automate that; stop rules are there for that reason.

When it does fit, you do not need a huge project. You need **one task** and an honest count of the hours it steals. We can look at that in thirty minutes.
